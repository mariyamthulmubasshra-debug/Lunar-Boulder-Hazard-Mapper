# Lunar Boulder Hazards and Landing-Site Assessment

## 1. Why boulders matter

Boulders can create hazards during lunar landings. A boulder beneath a landing leg may cause spacecraft tilt, while large rocks can damage a lander. Boulder distribution and size are therefore relevant to landing-site assessment.

Source URL: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20160012744

## 2. What makes a landing area suitable?

A landing area should ideally have relatively smooth and level terrain, limited large boulders, and no dangerous slopes, cliffs, or craters. A complete assessment requires more than identifying rocks.

Source URL: https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/3587/

## 3. How NASA evaluates landing hazards

NASA's lunar landing research describes using terrain information, imaging, and hazard-detection systems. NASA's SPLICE technology includes a Hazard Detection Lidar that creates three-dimensional terrain maps to identify hazards such as steep slopes and large rocks.

Source URL: https://www.nasa.gov/safe-and-precise-landing-integrated-capabilities-evolution-splice/

## 4. Why boulder size and distribution matter

The number, size, and spatial distribution of boulders can help characterize potential landing hazards. A region with fewer detected boulders may have a lower estimated boulder burden, but this alone does not establish that it is safe.

## 5. Limitations of image-based boulder detection

Image-based detection depends on image resolution, illumination, shadows, surface appearance, and model performance. Without image scale information, object dimensions should be reported in pixels rather than metres.

## 6. Limitations of this project

Lunar Boulder Hazard Mapper focuses on boulder detection and relative boulder burden. It does not independently evaluate terrain slope, surface strength, subsurface conditions, illumination, spacecraft landing-leg geometry, or all mission constraints.

Its output is decision-support information, not a certified safe-landing recommendation.
